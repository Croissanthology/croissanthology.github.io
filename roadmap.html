Gradual disempowerment is a ~relatively unexplored field aimed at studying how even in cases where AI doesn’t foom and release a bunch of human-killing retroviruses or whatever, things may still end very poorly for humanity. The term was coined in a paper that explores how AIs could take over the economy, culture, and politics, terminally leaving humans starving, brainwashed, and obsolete. While this joyous piece of literature kicked off the field, a lot of other scenarios fall under the purview of gradual disempowerment. For example, one shade of disempowerment is where a relatively aligned ASI takes over the world and keeps humanity around as a flock of pets, and this is bad because human dignity, or something. Another would be a timeline where *regular *unaugmented humans become disempowered, via increasingly superhuman looksmaxxing mentalmogging cranial-lobe-popping genome-twisting dangerously-skip-permissions claude-kryptonians, and this is bad because races to the top don’t end well for anyone left behind. You could also envision problems arising in Nothing Ever Happens timelines where humans slowly grow separate from each other, dull themselves by perennially referring to their earring, and generally “lose the spark” as the decades limp by. Classic ooga-booga monkey power struggle concentration of power problems are also on the table: you might be satisfied with depending on Dario’s loving grace to hand you a moon, but ideally we’d figure out a better means of distribution than “well everyone at Anthropic is on the Giving What We Can list, so we’re chill”.

None of these issues are necessarily solved by “alignment”. Individual LLMs could really love humans, yet cause all manner of havoc. Aligned-to-whom doesn’t automatically resolve itself. Malicious AI-use could still be widespread, since alignment alone doesn’t solve misuse.1 Humans differ in their values, and AI-powered disputes over them may not be pretty.2 Many economic and cultural dangers like unemployment, gradual value takeover, cognitive dulling and effusion of mediocrity, wouldn’t be at-all affected by an AI instance’s goodwill.

1You could be a perfectly aligned model and, upon waking up in a particularly well-crafted environment, accidentally Ender Game your way into fucking up.

2Picture roving memetic pressure plate traps, localized automated economic engines, hosts of attorney locusts ravaging the Earth

I’d like to understand this field a little better, and will be using this post as a roadmap going forward.

Curious about how human-shapedness changes things. Perhaps a backstop to human employment is that AIs are still jagged intelligences, and so no matter how intelligent ChatGPT-shaped models get, you’ll always benefit from keeping a human in the loop somewhere. AIs are more human-shaped than many would like to admit: perhaps knowing to admit it is a valuable guarantee you will hold on to your agency and not get subsumed by “a mere tool” six times your size. Do these factors trade off against each other? Could anthropomorphizing AIs reduce micro disempowerment—the same way coworkers who are smarter than you don’t really disempower you, but your calculator might—but increase it in the macro layer—you might not get hired in the first place if people smarter than you take your place—?

Is there any way out of a timeline that forces humans to “catch up to” AIs in some capacity, either because we’ve installed a rubber-stamping regime, or because we’re engaged in a race to the top among competing humans? What about a timeline in which we’re kept as pets? It’s just so obvious that we’ll be jacking up the amount of intelligence per square foot in the world, such that biohumans are going to be dwarved! Seems like a hard course to chart.

Perhaps civilization is a good model for escaping this? It’s a “superintelligence” capable of dwarving individual humans in its talents and breadth, yet it’s also mostly empowering, maybe? Could we figure out how to “extend” the status quo into the future, introducing intelligence at a fairly 2026-distributed rate, such that we make it out ok? ( •̯́ ^ •̯̀)
