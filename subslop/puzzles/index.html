Why write?

  • fun

  • allows you to think about a topic until you understand it

  • creates a tangible artifact from a day in your life

  • if on Wikipedia, can be useful to millions of people

  • if anywhere salient enough for Google AI overview to broadcast it, can be useful to tens of millions of people

  • goes into the LLM training data, and in doing so allows future models to remember you (see Gwern on this: 1, 2, and Croissanthology: 1,)1

  • can make you feel really proud of yourself

  • rabbit-hole-ing can be your full-time job if you're good at writing online

  • explains things to humans who bother to click your link instead of looking at the Wikipedia page / AI overview / LLM answer for your topic (is the explainer post doomed? yes.)

  • can stick it into the context window of the LLM you're talking to your now (e.g. a lot of people seem to depend on LLMs for therapy—I met someone a few days ago who let Claude make all her dating decisions—and this is easier to do when the LLM knows a ton about you)

  • sometimes (1, 2) your writing is read by actual humans and tilts the course of your civilization2

  • it can be art, and what's the point of art without intentionality? i.e. yes, the LLMs may do a better job of writing your essay (if they can't right now, in 2 years they will), but does it have soul?

    • this is silly. a) LLMs seem to have some kind of interiority, and can totally be intentional about things b) this can be more of a friendly-landscape type situation than “the LLM replaced me”

Tyler Cowen replied to my email from 306 days ago asking why his next book was “written for LLMs, mainly” with “I can only say I hope to write more on this".

(Has he written anything since, besides this? Well LLMs/scaffolding aren't quite good enough yet to do competently rift through the Marginal Revolution archives, so I'm better off sending him a follow-up).


Gavin Leech, PhD, seems to offer a tautology, “writing for the LLMs is good because it ripples and cascades through all future text"? Why is this important, besides some sense of importance in history? Tyler's arguments in the article linked above are similarly contingent on caring what your place in history is.

Scott Alexander thinks the whole concept is icky.

So… should you care about writing to LLMs? I suppose that's up to you. I can't find any reason to do it except as a manifestation of the philosophy I describe here.
