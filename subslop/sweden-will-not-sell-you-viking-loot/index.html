The econ-brained miss much of how the world works. For example, that the mechanism behind priceless things (like “sovereignty”) is the same mechanism that powers dollar bills. This leads to grimly hilarious results, like Tyler Cowen arguing an unaligned superintelligence would want to “trade with us” because something-something everyone wants to make a profit. The world's CEVs are not composed of a substrate of dollar bills! It's the opposite. Trade is but one option on a gameboard run by the human CEV, which includes such things as “not letting other societies kill all your society's people” and “serving everyone's moral agenda” that do not always respond well to being priced. Priceless things are why prices were invented, not the reverse.

It may seem surprising at first that this couple isn't being paid 1.8 million dollars! That the incentive for finding historical artifacts is a mere 25% cut. But it makes sense when you consider “a lot of things are priceless".

Culture, for one, is priceless. The national narrative of Sweden, which includes the viking period, is backstopped by items on display in museums whose access is considered a right for Swedish citizens. The idea that these coins might end up at a private auction is widely considered sad compared to the alternative where classroom field trips can admire them on display.1

Does that justify nabbing it from those who found the treasure? Well, the loot was never theirs: it was Bjørg Half-Eye's when he buried it there, and then his descendants scattered to the winds and the legal system changed. But if no-one can lay claim on them, why would it go to the state? Because the state is the Schelling point for collective morality. After enough time the coins gained more cultural value than they had inherent value from their silver. And who owns culture? “Us". And who gets to incarnate “us"?
